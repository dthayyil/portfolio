Most Copilot rollouts stall at "we turned it on." Seats get assigned, usage is
uneven, and leadership can't answer the only question that matters: *is this
making us better?*

## Three pillars of governed enablement

1. **Standards** — shared custom instructions, prompt patterns and repo-level
   configuration so AI output matches your architecture.
2. **Guardrails** — security and policy review of AI-generated changes, ideally
   automated in CI.
3. **Metrics** — adoption, acceptance rate, lead-time and quality signals tied to
   business outcomes.

## Measuring what matters

Vanity metrics (suggestions shown) tell you nothing. Track **accepted-and-shipped**
changes, review rework, and cycle time. Pair quantitative signals with developer
sentiment.

```ts
// Example: a lightweight adoption metric surfaced to leadership
type CopilotSignal = {
  team: string;
  acceptanceRate: number; // accepted / shown
  leadTimeDelta: number;  // vs. baseline, in hours
  reviewRework: number;   // % of AI PRs needing significant rework
};
```

Governed enablement turns Copilot from a novelty into a compounding productivity
advantage — one you can actually defend in a business case.
